WorkIndia - OTP Onboarding & Activation

3m read
Activation Funnel Diagnosis Vendor Strategy

Verification was the biggest activation drop-off - so logins skip OTP entirely on a saved token, and registrations run a four-channel fallback ladder.

96.3%OTP success
96.6%5-sec delivery
98.2%login segment
1Diagnose 2Ladder 3Tokens 42nd vendor

Problem

Every candidate had to clear phone verification before doing anything else, and a meaningful share never got past it. Delivery-latency and user-behaviour analysis both pointed at OTP as the single largest activation bottleneck.

Hypothesis

Failed verifications were a delivery-channel sequencing problem, not a user-intent one. Recover people on a second channel before they give up, and activation rises with no extra acquisition spend.

Approach

  • Split the funnel by channel, vendor, and time-to-deliver to separate "never delivered" from "delivered too late" - identical in a conversion chart, opposite fixes.
  • Built a progressive recovery ladder - Primary SMS → WhatsApp → Secondary SMS → OTP Call - each rung firing on the previous one's failure, ordered by delivery success, then latency, then user effort.
  • Added saved login tokens so returning users re-authenticate in one tap (the Instagram / Flipkart / Zepto pattern), with a fixed TTL after which they re-enter the OTP funnel - plus a secondary SMS vendor for redundancy.

Key Decisions

  • Sequence by expected success, not cost: a failed cheap send costs a user.
  • Returning users shouldn't verify at all: removing the step beat optimising it - 98.2% on the login segment, ~56% of all volume.
  • 5-second delivery as its own guardrail: otherwise the funnel gets "fixed" by simply retrying more.
  • A second vendor before a smarter algorithm: redundancy solved a failure class no routing logic could, and shipped faster.

Impact

OTP success rate89.6%96.3%+6.7pp

SegmentVolume weightBeforeAfterΔ
Login~56%89.6%98.2%+8.6pp

Why "before" is identical across segments: everyone walked the same funnel - enter mobile → OTP delivered → enter code → verify - on a single vendor, inheriting its throughput issues with no fallback. Segment couldn't matter, because the path didn't differ.

Why they diverge after: the two segments now take different paths.

  • Login (~56% of volume) re-authenticates on a saved token in one tap - the pattern Instagram, Flipkart, Zepto, and BigBasket use. No OTP is sent, so vendor delivery stops being a factor at all.
  • Register (~44%) still goes through the OTP funnel - but the funnel itself got better, because the fallback ladder recovers the sends the primary vendor drops.
  • Tokens carry a fixed TTL, so a login user returning after it expires lands back in the OTP funnel - and picks up the same ladder improvement as a registering user.

What I'd Do Next

  • Route the first attempt by carrier and geography, and explore silent network-based verification so eligible users never see an OTP screen.
  • Measure activation-to-first-apply - verifying more users only matters if they go on to apply.

WorkIndia - Job Discovery & Apply Conversion

4m read
Conversion User Research Behavioural Analytics Ranking

The survey said WhatsApp, and the CTA won +30% - but apply rate was still short, so I built a click heatmap and found the real cause.

+30%Lands-to-Apply
+6%applies (density)
+8.1%applies (ranking)
1Ask 2Ship 3Heatmap 4Fix

Problem

Apply rate on job cards was underperforming and nobody knew why - no established link to pagination, layout, or ranking, just a weak number. So the first step was finding out what candidates were actually trying to do on that screen.

First Move: Ask the Users

A preference survey on how candidates want to reach employers came back clear: 45% preferred a WhatsApp CTA against 18% who would call a recruiter directly - a mismatch with the calling-first flow we pushed them into.

Shipped a WhatsApp HR CTA on job cards with design and engineering. It worked: +30% Lands-to-Apply.

Still Short: Build the Heatmap

The CTA won and apply rate was still below target - the survey had found a problem, not the problem. So I built a click heatmap from raw event logs, mapping engagement by position on the listing page:

  • WhatsApp CTA taps were healthy - ruling the new surface out as the bottleneck.
  • Clicks concentrated in the top cards, with sharp fall-off down the page.
  • Users reached pagination fast - scanning, not evaluating.
  • Pagination CTR ran ~2x the Apply rate - they were paging past our listings.

Hypothesis

Now there was a hypothesis worth testing: users paged past listings because there were too few per screen and the ordering was wrong. A discovery problem - not job supply, and no longer the CTA.

Approach

  • Benchmarked competitor listing pages, then raised density from 10 to 20 jobs per page - testing the "too few per screen" half on its own.
  • With density held constant, shipped custom relevancy-ranking logic to test the ordering half independently.

Key Decisions

  • Ask before assuming: with no known cause, talking to users beat redesigning on instinct.
  • Don't let a win close the investigation: +30% made it easy to declare victory - the apply-rate metric said otherwise.
  • Heatmap over aggregates: funnel totals say "conversion is low"; position-level data says "ordering is wrong". Only one is actionable.
  • Lands-to-Apply, not taps: a CTA tapped without producing applications adds noise for employers.

What I'd Do Next

  • Make the heatmap a standing instrument, so the next ordering regression surfaces on its own.
  • Ranking weights per segment, plus apply-intent prediction and recruiter response SLA as ranking signals.

WorkIndia - CLM Channel Efficiency & Lifecycle Growth

Growth Bandits Cost Efficiency Lifecycle

Spend was outgrowing DAU, so send-time became a contextual bandit rewarded on applications rather than opens - plus algorithm reuse and a new email channel.

−23%cost per DAU
+17%DAU

1Analyse 2Bandit 3Reuse 4Email

Problem

CLM spend was growing faster than the DAU it produced. Campaigns optimised for opens - a metric that rewards sending more, more often - so blended cost-per-DAU climbed while heavily-messaged users disengaged.

Hypothesis

Send timing, algorithm reuse, and channel mix are bigger levers than volume. Optimise each user's send moment against downstream value, reuse the logic that already works, and add a genuinely cheap channel.

Approach

  • Hourly behavioural, cohort, and notification-level analysis pinpointed send timing as the dominant efficiency driver.
  • Grew the Notification Time Affinity Model into a contextual Thompson Sampling system - picking each send window against downstream value, bounded by fatigue-aware caps.
  • Traced why in-app recommendation beat retargeting, then had engineering reuse the stronger algorithm across both via Kafka.
  • Launched Email re-engagement at ₹0.17 cost-per-DAU, segmented under-engaged cohorts into lifecycle initiatives, and shipped benchmarked notification design patterns.

Key Decisions

  • Reward = applications, not opens: a bandit optimises what you pay it for, and opens teach it to spam.
  • Frequency caps as a hard constraint: the cap bounds exploration instead of competing with it inside the reward.
  • Reuse the proven algorithm: the performance gap was explained - the work was event plumbing, not modelling.
  • Reallocate spend, don't cut it: efficiency went back into reach, so cheaper DAU didn't mean fewer DAU.

Engineered Systems

One system, matured in two stages: per-user engagement-window scoring, grown into a contextual bandit. Arms are send-hour bands; context is segment, channel, recent engagement; reward is downstream application and retention value. Fatigue caps bound exploration per user.

Key decision: the rule-based affinity scores became the bandit's prior, so cold-start users get a sensible window instead of random exploration.

Impact: −23% Cost-per-DAU, ₹1.2L infra saved.

One pipeline, two stages. Auto-refresh: every 14 days, recompute time-affinity and engagement over the trailing 30 days and re-bucket any user whose window shifted >20%, logging changes for audit. Copy generation: the refreshed segment profile plus job category feeds an LLM pipeline producing on-brand variants with character-limit guards, injected into the campaign scheduler.

Key decision: copy regenerates because segments moved - refreshing the audience while the message stays frozen was the failure mode this removes.

Impact: read-rates +15.6%, clicks +4%, click-to-install +9.2%.

Star schema with a notifications fact table (one row per event), dimensions for time, channel, segment, and job category, and pre-built aggregators for the L0 funnel (sent → delivered → read → clicked → applied). Built because cost and engagement numbers disagreed depending on who pulled them.

Key decision: raw events in the fact table, not pre-aggregated - so new metric definitions don't require schema changes.

What I'd Do Next

  • Extend the bandit to cross-channel budget allocation - same reward signal deciding WhatsApp vs push vs email.
  • Add a permanent no-contact holdout for incrementality, and learn fatigue caps per segment instead of setting them globally.

WorkIndia - Acquisition Funnel Experimentation

2m read
Experimentation Statistics Acquisition

A 1M+ daily visitor funnel was being changed on intuition, so I installed a hypothesis → power → guardrail loop and gated rollout on significance.

+6.2%Clicks-to-Leads
1M+daily visitors
Stat-sigrollout gate
1Hypothesise 2Power 3Test 4Gate

Problem

A funnel serving 1M+ daily visitors was being changed on intuition. Wins were declared from directional movement and losses explained away, with no shared standard for what counted as evidence.

Hypothesis

At this traffic volume, a disciplined loop - stated hypothesis, powered sample size, pre-declared guardrails - surfaces real lift that eyeballing dashboards misses, and stops us shipping changes that only look positive.

Approach

  • Owned experimentation strategy: which hypotheses were worth testing and in what order, on an effort-vs-impact read.
  • Set primary, secondary, and guardrail metrics before each launch (primary: Clicks-to-Leads), and sized every test for the effect it needed to detect - so a null meant "no effect worth having", not "not enough data".
  • Validated +6.2% Clicks-to-Leads through statistically significant A/B tests, feeding straight into the rollout decision.

Key Decisions

  • Clicks-to-Leads as primary: closest to business value here - upstream clicks are easy to inflate and prove nothing.
  • Pre-declare success criteria: what separates an experiment from a search for a favourable slice.
  • Gate on significance, not direction: a positive-looking underpowered result ships risk, not lift.
  • One change per test: no bundling, so every shipped change had an attributable effect size.

What I'd Do Next

  • Sequential testing to shorten cycles without inflating false positives from repeated peeking.
  • An experiment registry so learnings compound, plus guardrail alerting during rollout to catch a winner that degrades something downstream.

Samsung Prism - LLM Safety Guardian

2m read
ML Systems NLP Anomaly Detection

Unexplained moderation flags were expensive to review - so every flag got an explanation grounded in the exact policy clause it allegedly violated.

86.3%accuracy
−40%false positives
32,000+documents
1Extract 2Classify 3Explain

Problem

Automated moderation generated too many false positives, overloading reviewers and delaying legitimate approvals. Reviewers couldn't see why something was flagged, so every borderline case cost a full manual read.

Hypothesis

The bottleneck wasn't classifier accuracy - it was that an unexplained flag is expensive to review. Attach a grounded, policy-cited explanation and reviewers dismiss weak flags in seconds, cutting the false-positive burden at the same accuracy.

Approach

  • 32,000+ documents through scalable extraction pipelines, feeding both the classifier and the retrieval index.
  • Fine-tuned Roberta-Large for policy violation classification (86.3% held out), plus transformer summarization tuned on accuracy, throughput, and deployment efficiency together.
  • Anomaly monitoring over 10K+ interactions via statistical baselines and sliding-window z-scores.
  • A RAG layer (Llama 3.2 1B) over a dense index of policy clauses, so each flag cited the clause it allegedly violated.

Key Decisions

  • Explainability over more accuracy: the last points of F1 cost more than making each flag cheap to adjudicate.
  • Ground explanations in retrieved policy text: a fluent hallucination launders a wrong flag into a convincing one.
  • A 1B base model on purpose: the explanation layer runs over every flag, so inference cost was a design constraint from the start.

Impact

86.3% Detection Accuracy
−40% False Positives
10K+ Interactions Monitored

What I'd Do Next

  • Retrain weekly on reviewer corrections - a human-in-the-loop feedback layer.
  • Route only borderline cases (0.4–0.6 confidence) to human review, auto-resolving the rest.

Experimentation & Growth Systems

Infrastructure that makes decisions repeatable, not one-off.

Notification Time Affinity Model (Contextual Thompson Sampling)

Send-time as a contextual bandit - rewarded on downstream value, bounded by fatigue caps.

  1. Arms: send-hour bands across the day.
  2. Context: segment, channel, recent engagement recency/frequency.
  3. Reward: applications and retention - explicitly not opens, so it can't win by learning to spam.
  4. Prior: rule-based engagement-window scores seed each posterior, so cold-start users skip random exploration.
  5. Constraint: fatigue caps bound exploration - a hard attention-budget limit, not a soft penalty in the reward.

Result: −23% Cost-per-DAU, ₹1.2L infra saved.

A/B Design on a 1M+ Visitor Funnel

Stated hypothesis, powered sample size, pre-declared guardrails, significance gate before rollout.

  1. Hypothesis as a falsifiable statement, not a goal - "X will raise Clicks-to-Leads because Y".
  2. Declare primary (Clicks-to-Leads), secondary, and guardrail metrics before starting.
  3. Size for the minimum effect worth shipping, so a null means "no meaningful effect", not "not enough traffic".
  4. One change per test, so every shipped change carries an attributable effect size.
  5. Gate rollout on significance, not on the direction the chart points.

Result: +6.2% Clicks-to-Leads.

Progressive Fallback Ladder (OTP)

Channel recovery as a measured sequence - each rung ordered by evidence, measured on its own.

  1. Order: Primary SMS → WhatsApp → Secondary SMS → OTP Call, by measured delivery success, then latency, then user effort.
  2. Each rung fires on the previous one's failure and is measured independently, so we know which rung actually recovers users.
  3. Guardrail: 5-second delivery tracked separately from eventual success - otherwise "success" is bought with slow retries.
  4. Saved login tokens remove the ladder entirely for identifiable returning users; a second SMS vendor adds redundancy no routing logic could.

Result: OTP success 89.6% → 96.3%; 5-sec delivery 88.2% → 96.6%.

Diagnose → Ship → Re-diagnose

The loop for when your first fix works and the metric still doesn't move.

  1. Ask: apply rate weak, no known cause - so survey candidates on how they want to reach employers.
  2. Signal: 45% preferred a WhatsApp CTA against 18% who would call directly.
  3. Ship & measure on behaviour: WhatsApp HR CTA on job cards, judged on Lands-to-Apply rather than taps - +30%.
  4. Re-diagnose: apply rate still short, so the survey found a problem, not the problem. A click heatmap from raw event logs showed healthy CTA taps, clicks concentrated in top cards, and pagination CTR ~2x the Apply rate.
  5. Then hypothesise and ship: density 10 → 20 jobs/page (+6%), then relevancy ranking (+8.1%).

A survey tells you what users say; a heatmap tells you what they do. This case needed both, in that order.

Composite & Efficiency Metric Design

Metrics built so a campaign can't win by sacrificing user trust, and so channels compare on one number.

Notification Health Score =

(0.4 x Click Rate) + (0.4 x Application Rate) - (0.2 x Unsubscribe Rate)

Campaigns can't win by trading long-term trust for short-term clicks.

Blended Cost-per-DAU sits above it as the cross-channel north star - total spend over the DAU it produces. It makes WhatsApp, push, and email directly comparable, and it's what exposed Email at ₹0.17 against ₹1.3 elsewhere.

Segment Refresh → Copy Regeneration Loop

A 14-day cycle that doesn't just re-bucket users - it regenerates the copy those users receive.

  1. Cron runs every 14 days, post-campaign cycle.
  2. Re-computes time-affinity and engagement scores from the last 30 days of events.
  3. Users whose engagement window drifted >20% are re-bucketed, with every change logged for audit.
  4. Then: the refreshed segment profile plus job category feeds the Agentic Copy Writer, which regenerates on-brand variants with character-limit guards, injected into the campaign scheduler.

The coupling is the point - refreshing the audience while the message stays frozen is the staleness this prevents.

How I Think About Products

Mental models I use to make better product decisions.

01

Optimise for downstream metrics, not vanity metrics

Open rate is a proxy; application rate is an outcome. I trace the metric chain to what actually matters, and guardrail the rest.

02

Balance engagement with user fatigue

More notifications ≠ more engagement. Every decision spends an attention budget, so I model unsubscribe cost alongside click gain.

03

Prefer systems over one-off solutions

A script that runs once is technical debt. A pipeline that reruns, logs itself, and handles edge cases is a product. I build for the second run.

04

Use data to guide, not dictate decisions

Data shows what happened, rarely why. I pair the numbers with behavioural context instead of outsourcing the decision to a metric.

05

Ship experiments, not assumptions

Every strong opinion is a hypothesis in disguise. Small fast experiments before large builds minimise the cost of being wrong.

06

Make the invisible legible

The best insight is the one nobody sees yet - buried in event logs, latency spikes, drop-off points. Data exploration is a product skill.
